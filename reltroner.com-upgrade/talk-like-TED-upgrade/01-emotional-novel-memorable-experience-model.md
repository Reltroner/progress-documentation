# 01 — Emotional, Novel, Memorable: Cognitive and Communication Experience Architecture

> Status: OWNER-APPROVED DESIGN PRINCIPLES / OUTCOMES UNVALIDATED  
> Inspiration: *Talk Like TED: The 9 Public-Speaking Secrets of the World's Top Minds*, Carmine Gallo. This is a design application, not a scholarly claim that every human remembers exactly three items.

## 1. The three anchors

| Anchor | Visitor experience | Editorial question | Usable proof | Anti-pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Emotional — Recognize** | A human desire, dilemma, or friction feels relevant | Why would this person care *now*? | Specific choice with stakes | Manipulative fear, generic inspiration, hollow awe |
| **Novel — Discover** | A causal relationship or perspective was unexpected yet intelligible | What became visible that was not obvious before? | Before/after with a traceable mechanism | Decorative novelty, invented pseudo-science, jargon as status |
| **Memorable — Retain & Reuse** | A person can explain one consequence and choose a meaningful next action | What can they take away without memorizing our taxonomy? | Concise insight, exercise output, story question | Slogans unsupported by action, forced endless CTAs |

**Three refers to the primary conceptual scaffold.** A task with six causal steps, eight course modules or three narrative modes can still be presented correctly; do not arbitrarily delete necessary content. Empirical capacity depends on task and person. This project must not claim “the brain is designed to remember only three things.”

## 2. Two audiences, one intellectual engine

**Creator:** Emotional = “I have ideas that do not connect”; Novel = see the rule/constraint/interaction generate a different world state; Memorable = “My previous decision can produce my next question,” together with their own artifact.

**Reader:** Emotional = identify with a human-level decision; Novel = show how institution and civilization alter that choice; Memorable = “Progress changes the dilemmas; it does not erase the need to choose,” without making a single fictional world the definitive answer.

One connected creative logic: idea → structure → interaction → consequence → changed state → new pressure/question → further possibility. The studio's public technical interfaces are carriers of this content, not independent product identity.

## 3. How the three anchors coexist with five communication levels

| Message depth | User need | Dominant anchor | Surface |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| L1 — Human hook | Why should I care? | Emotional | Hero headline, scene opening |
| L2 — Recognizable problem | Is this my situation? | Emotional | One specific friction or choice |
| L3 — Value proposition | What changes for me? | Novel | One meaningful mechanism in plain language |
| L4 — Worked method | How exactly? | Novel → Memorable | Six-step exercise, scene/Backstory/Wiki or state transition |
| L5 — Source architecture | Can I examine/verify? | Memorable via evidence | WOS lesson, source of truth, annotated archive |

The five levels are communication resolution, **not** WOS modules, narrative modes or a site navigation rewrite. Do not force users through every level; show the shallowest useful level and offer deeper context voluntarily.

## 4. Communication objective

For any first-contact block, design: **Relevance → Mechanism → Evidence/Limit → Invitation**.

- **Relevance:** problem described in language the intended audience already recognizes, explicitly hypothetical if not user-reported.
- **Mechanism:** state what changes, why, and for whom. Connect consequences instead of listing categories.
- **Evidence/limit:** link an inspectable artifact or label a teaching demonstration as hypothetical; do not advertise general learning efficacy from one fictional example.
- **Invitation:** one actually available next action (“Try,” “Explore,” “Read,” “Contact”) without implying “Buy/Book/Enroll” where unavailable.

**Editorial test:** after reading a section, can a new visitor tell a causal story with “because / therefore / what changed?” If not, refine information architecture before adding spectacle.

## 5. Three candidate brand-facing beats

These are **draft experience signatures**, not canon names or automatic homepage replacements.

1. **One Idea.** Begin with real curiosity.
2. **Meaningful Consequences.** Show decisions propagating to people, systems or stories.
3. **New Possibilities.** Let an outcome become a new beginning.

Optional bridge copy: **“Feel the question. Discover the system. Remember what changed.”**

Use the signature selectively. A repetition at every scroll section would turn it into visual noise. Translating it into Indonesian should preserve implications, not word-for-word rhythm.

## 6. Ethical differentiation and accessibility

Do not create emotion by exploiting anxiety, claims of imminent failure or artificial scarcity. Avoid novelty that creates a confusing or false distinction. A memorable thought must be supported by evidence appropriate to its claim. Provide text alternatives to any animated causal diagram and keep motion non-essential.

## 7. Example A — Creator, lighthouse

**Emotional:** “A harbor town has a lighthouse, but two communities need light at incompatible times.”  
**Novel:** A limited resource causes negotiations, institutions or customs; one rule changes social behavior.  
**Memorable:** **Constraints can create possibilities**. The next question must inherit a specific prior change.

Give the creator a usable seed, constraint, agent choice, outcome and new question; do not promise their entire world is automatically solved.

## 8. Example B — Reader, observatory

**Emotional:** A researcher notices a possible issue before a much-awaited opening; speaking up may cost trust or reputation.  
**Novel:** Her decision can affect teams, inter-civilizational cooperation and institutional accountability.  
**Memorable:** A technological breakthrough does not relieve people of judgment.

This is a **hypothetical non-canon teaching scene**, not an announced Asthortera episode, published event or representative claim about every civilization.

## 9. Measurement stance

Use a first-visit comprehension task (can the person explain the studio and two paths?), a causal recall task (what changed and why?), and a voluntary next-action task (can the visitor choose the appropriate route?). Record actual responses and uncertainty; **no invented conversion, memory-retention or engagement uplift**.
