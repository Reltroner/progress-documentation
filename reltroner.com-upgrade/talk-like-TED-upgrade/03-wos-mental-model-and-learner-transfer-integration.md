# 03 — Worldbuilding Operating System Integration: Usage, Mental Models and Bounded Transfer

> Status: ACCEPTED UX ALIGNMENT WITH WOS CANDIDATE v0.3 — **NOT** WOS course ratification or learner-outcome proof.  
> WOS source: progress-documentation/worldbuilding-operating-system, revised 24 October 2026.

## 1. Why WOS changes the website proposition

WOS is proposed as beginner-first, creator-owned practice in **systemic creative architecture**: fictional worldbuilding serves as a low-stakes laboratory for causal reasoning and problem framing. The value is not learning an encyclopedia about Asthortera, mastering software, or gaining a universally transferable professional competence.

The website should show a useful creative decision first, then name the underlying mental model. **Usage before jargon; consequence before classification; agency before mentorship.**

## 2. Four-part educational interface contract

| Layer | Learner-facing explanation | UX evidence | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Use case** | “I have a world idea but don't know what follows.” | One specific choice/problem at stake | Relevance remains a hypothesis if not learner-reported |
| **Mental model** | “A constraint reshapes available choices and consequences.” | One abstract rule in ordinary words | Naming a pattern is not proof of independent ability |
| **Worldbuilding artifact** | “My world now contains a changed rule/state and a new question.” | Own notes/export, optional local worksheet | Story artifact alone proves no real-world transfer |
| **Bounded transfer probe** | “Might this reasoning pattern apply to a low-risk everyday choice?” | Map/mismatch/evidence/alternative/reversible test | Optional; success requires separate observation |

Optional feedback: “What does not map between this fictional situation and the real one?” The user may correctly reject an analogy.

## 3. Reuse existing six-stage Creative Compound exercise

1. Seed — one meaningful world idea.
2. Constraint — one limit, trade-off or rule.
3. Interaction — one affected actor or system.
4. Consequence — what follows causally.
5. Changed state — what persists afterward (S0 → S1).
6. New question — an inherited next seed.

The longer Studio Master loop includes system, new pressure, new possibility and further interaction. The shorter six-stage UI is **learner-oriented progressive disclosure**, not a replacement constitution.

**Success trace:** the learner can name which prior changed state caused the new question. Do not reward merely appending unrelated lore.

## 4. Fully illustrative, noncanonical worked example

**Seed:** a town depends on a lighthouse.  
**Constraint:** it can illuminate only one harbor corridor at a time.  
**Interaction:** two communities request conflicting schedules.  
**Consequence:** a public allocation agreement is established; some residents challenge its fairness.  
**Changed state:** the town now has an institution and a remembered controversy.  
**New question:** who may revise the agreement after an emergency?

**Mental model:** scarcity/constraint → conflicting incentives → collective rule → institutional feedback.  
**Possible low-risk analogy:** scheduling a shared study room.  
**What maps:** resources, timing, alternative options.  
**What does NOT map:** fictional laws, consent and culture cannot establish real group rules.  
**Evidence needed:** availability, actual permission, affected participants and alternative schedules.  
**Possible test:** ask stakeholders and trial a reversible schedule, if consented.

The example is a **teaching scenario, not official Asthortera canon, not field evidence and not an efficacy study**.

## 6. Evidence ladder W / M / N / X

- **W — World usage:** a creator reasons about an original fictional decision with traceable effects.
- **M — Mental-model expression:** the creator explains the structural pattern without fictional proper nouns.
- **N — Near transfer:** the creator independently adapts the procedure to another fictional setting or genre.
- **X — Bounded cross-domain attempt:** the creator justifies use/rejection of a selected pattern in an unfamiliar, low-risk non-fiction problem, checks mismatches and evidence, and proposes a reversible test.

W does **not** establish M, N, or X; passing a quiz or a compelling story is not proof of transfer. Long-term benefit and occupational competence remain outside current evidence.

## 7. Transfer safeguards

Never imply fictional policy establishes actual economics, medicine, law, politics or engineering safety; never mandate high-stakes transfer. Real people have actual constraints, consent rights, uncertainty and domain-specific evidence. No claims of guaranteed productivity, success, wellbeing, publication or professional qualification.

**Non-fiction procedure:** name real problem → abstract useful form → check mapping/mismatches → seek external evidence → compare alternatives → try a reversible low-risk step or defer → update the model.

## 8. Website ↔ LMS contract

- Website introduces one or two **real, inspectable methods** and offers **optional** LMS continuation.
- LMS retains independent identity, auth/authorization, content status, infrastructure, publication gates, learner assessment and operational source authority. No shared mutable learner state is introduced.
- Deep link to a lesson only after verifying its actual guest access, publication and relevance. If unavailable, link to existing public course landing material or omit; never invent shipped course depth.
- CreatorNotebook may generate a portable Markdown prompt. Content must not be automatically submitted to third-party AI; AI use and data transfer remain an explicit user action with proper disclosure.
- Personal artifacts must remain creator-owned; feedback/testimonial use requires separate voluntary permission.

## 9. Educational acceptance hypothesis

A first-time creator can complete one micro-experiment, explain one mechanism in their own words, and identify what would be needed before cautiously applying it elsewhere. Test the three claims **independently**, not as a single aggregate score.
