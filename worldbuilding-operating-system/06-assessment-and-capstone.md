# 06 — Assessment, Capstone and Acceptance Design

**Status:** PROPOSED — 2026-10-14  
**Role:** Define how to evaluate **transferable creator capability**, not mere lore volume.

## Assessment principles

- Evaluate **reasoning evidenced by artifacts**, not popularity, complexity, genre, prose polish or imaginary GDP.
- Distinguish conceptual quiz results from practical performance.
- Give beginner-specific, bounded feedback and opportunities to revise.
- A learner may satisfy criteria using a small city, village, room, community or other localized world.
- No publication, public sharing, camera, payment or mentor attendance is required.
- Rubric values below are **design proposals**, not an approved grading or certification rule.

## Proposed capstone brief

**Title:** My Worldbuilding Operating System v1.0

Submit one coherent world package assembling all eight module artifacts. Then introduce a second creative cycle whose **input is an explicit consequence from the first cycle**, not an unrelated new lore fact. Include a short self-explanation of how world rules, actors and systems interact and what will happen next if a key condition changes.

## Proposed capstone evidence checklist

1. One bounded world premise with an explicit goal and scope.
2. At least two understandable world rules or conditions with constraints.
3. At least one inhabited location and recognizable everyday experience.
4. At least two systems linked by a directed causal relationship.
5. One initial state and a documented change event/decision.
6. At least one first-order and one plausible downstream consequence.
7. A reader-facing output that communicates a meaningful world relationship.
8. Clear separation of established facts, draft ideas and open contradictions.
9. One second cycle demonstrably inheriting a first-cycle outcome.
10. A self-directed next question and plan that does not require the instructor.

These are **observable candidate acceptance conditions**, not learner data or a frozen LMS rubric.

## Proposed quality weighting

| Dimension | Weight | Strong evidence | Weak evidence |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| World foundations and consistency | 20% | Rules and exceptions explain observable outcomes | Rules conflict or change arbitrarily |
| Agents and lived experience | 15% | Rules shape choices and daily life | Inhabitants are decorative |
| Systems and causal reasoning | 25% | Two systems influence each other via explained mechanisms | Categories merely sit beside each other |
| World change and compounding | 25% | State transition has durable inherited consequences | Event does not affect future possibilities |
| Documentation, clarity and independence | 15% | Another person can navigate the model; learner can extend it | Untraceable facts or mentor-dependent next step |
| **Total** | **100%** | | |

### Four-level descriptor pattern (draft)

- **Level 0 — Not evidenced:** absent or impossible to inspect.
- **Level 1 — Emerging:** basic element exists but causal link is unclear.
- **Level 2 — Demonstrated:** coherent, legible, justified and connected at beginner scope.
- **Level 3 — Strong transfer:** anticipates trade-offs, tests alternatives and independently reuses the mechanism.

Do not equate higher-level sophistication with encyclopedic size. Proposed numeric grade calculation, pass marks, resubmission policy and accessibility accommodation rules remain to be ratified.

## Quiz blueprint

Each module quiz should sample:

- **Concept recognition:** distinguish terms and identify appropriate examples.
- **Scenario interpretation:** infer what changes when an input/constraint changes.
- **Error diagnosis:** spot a contradiction, missing dependency or unjustified claim.
- **Transfer:** apply a principle to a new fictional genre or scale.

Avoid “Which exact phrase appeared in the slide?” as the dominant format. Quiz questions must be authored and reviewed separately.

## Practical review workflow

Evidence capture → check rubric → highlight strongest causal link → identify one meaningful gap → let learner revise → compare before/after → document remaining uncertainty.

## Evidence, not marketing

Completion does not prove reduced burnout, guaranteed creative productivity, successful publishing, reader engagement or market validation. The studio should validate learning outcomes with novice trials and disclosed methods before making effectiveness claims.
