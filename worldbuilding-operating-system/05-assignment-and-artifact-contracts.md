# 05 — Cumulative Assignments and Learner Artifact Contracts

**Status:** PROPOSED — 2026-10-14  
**Role:** Convert abstract lessons into independently inspectable learner work.

## Cumulative project rule

**One learner-selected world across all eight assignments.** Revision of earlier artifacts is encouraged; restarting on each module is not required. Learners own their original world and may keep all drafts private.

| Module | Deliverable | Required evidence | Feeds into |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 01 | Creator Direction Brief | Interest, goal, genre freedom, size preference, example reflection | Seed selection |
| 02 | World Seed & Premise | What-if premise, central question, initial scope, exclusions | Baseline rules |
| 03 | World Foundations Sheet | Starting conditions, at least two coherent rules, one limit and documented exception/edge case | Daily life |
| 04 | Entities & Lived Experience | One location, at least one agent/group, motivation, routine linked to one rule | System actors |
| 05 | World System Interaction Map | Two named systems, inputs/constraints, directed influence and causal explanation | Event selection |
| 06 | World Change Map | State before, event/decision, first/second-order consequences, changed state, new question | Reader-facing stakes |
| 07 | World Experience Artifact | Short scene, travelogue, in-world record or explanation; intended audience and knowledge need | Documented world |
| 08 | World Source of Truth & Creative Loop Log | Known rules, status labels, inconsistency notes, second cycle and next independent step | Final capstone |

## Paired evidence inside the same eight assignments (candidate v0.3)

Keep **one cumulative fictional world** and the existing **eight assignments**; do not create eight additional mandatory submissions. For each assignment, the fictional-world artifact remains primary. Add a short **reasoning annotation** that identifies the model and one potential outside-world application. Independent non-fiction transfer is assessed only in the separate capstone lane.

| Module | Visible reasoning habit | Guided non-fiction bridge (no sensitive information required) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 01 | Distinguish an interesting fact from a meaningful causal relationship | Identify a similar decision in a routine; note one uncertainty |
| 02 | Select problem boundaries and define an actionable question | Convert a vague hobby or learning goal into a bounded problem |
| 03 | Challenge assumptions and identify trade-offs | Adjust a shared activity plan to one genuine constraint |
| 04 | Compare stakeholder experience rather than presume sameness | Examine two perspectives on a team agreement; ask what should be verified |
| 05 | Map dependencies and possible bottlenecks | Trace a simple project handoff and a missing prerequisite |
| 06 | Compare alternative interventions and downstream consequences | Predict outcomes of two low-risk learning-schedule changes |
| 07 | Translate complex material into audience-relevant language | Explain a small process change and ask the audience what was understood |
| 08 | Independently choose, test and revise a reasoning procedure | Analyze one unseen everyday case; propose reversible next action or deferral |

**Minimum reasoning annotation:** `creative decision / model used / actual effect / possible outside context / what does not transfer / missing evidence`. Modules 01–02 may remain observational; 03–07 are scaffolded; Module 08 includes independent selection. Learners need not upload private work, medical, financial or personal data. A hypothetical everyday case is acceptable.

**Real-world validity:** a fictional explanation may be logically coherent yet empirically false if transplanted to reality. Always distinguish invented rules from observations; note affected people, missing evidence, and when to avoid action. ## Proposed assignment template

**Assignment ID/title:**  
**World/work-in-progress identifier:**  
**Input:** which previous work is inherited?  
**Creative decision:** what is intentionally being changed or added?  
**Rules/constraints:** which world conditions govern it?  
**Artifact:** prose, bullet list, map, diagram, audio with transcript or equivalent accessible format.  
**Causal proof:** explain what leads to what and why.  
**Uncertainty:** open questions or hypotheses, not falsely final facts.  
**Reflection:** what would be revised next?
**Mental-model note (as appropriate to module):** what reasoning pattern was used, what might transfer, what mismatches and what new evidence would be required?  
**Status:** draft / structured / ready for feedback.

## Deliverable-specific checks

### Assignment 01 — Discover Your Worldbuilding Direction

Learner identifies an interest, reflects on why a reference world is meaningful **without copying its canon**, and chooses a manageable learning goal.

### Assignment 02 — Create Your World Seed

Premise is explainable in a short paragraph. It poses a generative question. It names boundaries rather than promising to create everything.

### Assignment 03 — Establish Your World's Foundations

Rules can be used to predict at least one likely consequence. Local exceptions are explicitly scoped rather than invented to escape any challenge.

### Assignment 04 — Create a Lived-In World

A character/community makes a meaningful choice within the world. Everyday practices visibly depend on at least one foundation rule.

### Assignment 05 — Connect Your World Systems

Map is legible as text if necessary: **system A → mechanism → system B → result**. At least one decision or constraint makes the interaction nontrivial.

### Assignment 06 — Simulate a World Change

Events are not arbitrary author interventions: causation is traced, benefits and costs are considered, and at least one persistent consequence remains.

### Assignment 07 — Present Your Fictional World

Output has a named reader question. It presents present events, relevant history, or stable world reference **as needed**. A short scene is acceptable; a novel is not required.

### Assignment 08 — Build Your Worldbuilding Workflow

Source of truth distinguishes facts, drafts and contradictions. Demonstrate a **second cycle** grounded in a first-cycle outcome, with a defensible next step.

This is an **example**, not a mandatory Git repository, folder structure, toolchain or upload format. One notebook, one accessible document or plain text is valid when the evidence remains inspectable.

## One-page six-stage creator practice

| Stage | Accessible learner prompt | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seed | What interests me? | One idea |
| Constraint | What limit makes it matter? | One rule |
| Interaction | Who/what meets? | One relationship |
| Consequence | What changes, and why? | One causal effect |
| Changed state | What stays different? | S0 → S1 |
| New question | What is now possible to ask? | One inherited creative input |

**Example (noncanonical):** memory lanterns → consent → archive and mediator conflict → disputed testimony → new review process → who may challenge that process? This is a worked example, not learner efficacy evidence. Each assignment should open with a relatable challenge, demonstrate a mechanism, specify an inspectable outcome, then offer one *optional* self-directed action. Do not demand an account, paid mentor, AI, publishing or private-data upload. Learner IP stays learner-owned.

## Instructor feedback protocol

Use **observation → causal question → revision option**. Example: “The city's transport rule says journeys are instantaneous, but a three-day commute drives this conflict. Is this a local exception or should the conflict use a different constraint?” Do not impose the instructor's fictional preferences or rewrite the learner's project by default.

## Privacy and author rights

Public posting must be opt-in. A learner may redact names and sensitive details or submit a private excerpt. Do not present learner-created IP as Reltroner Studio canon; keep permission and attribution explicit.
