# 04 — Complete Course Syllabus (Candidate v0.1)

**Status:** PROPOSED — 2026-10-14  
**Role:** Preserve every proposed lesson title, module rationale, outcome, assignment and quiz for handoff.

This document is a **content inventory**, not complete teaching materials. Lesson scripts, illustrations, reading/video/audio assets, item banks, expected durations and LMS publication status are not yet authored or accepted.

**Title:** Worldbuilding Operating System  
**Subtitle:** Build Your Own Fictional World Through Systems Thinking  
**Target:** Beginner creators; original and learner-owned world; paper/Markdown accepted  
**Progression:** Understand → Imagine → Define → Populate → Connect → Simulate → Express → Operate.

## 01 — Worldbuilding Fundamentals

**Reasoning:** Establish a mental model before isolated vocabulary, aesthetics or lore lists.
**Main objective:** Understand worldbuilding as the creation of coherent, interconnected fictional worlds.

### Lessons

- **01.01 — What Is Worldbuilding?**
- **01.02 — Worldbuilding vs Storytelling vs Lore**
- **01.03 — The Worldbuilding Landscape** — Fantasy, science fiction, alternate history, speculative fiction and other original worlds.
- **01.04 — Why Fictional Worlds Need Internal Logic**
- **01.05 — Understanding Worldbuilding Scale** — Individual, community, civilization and world; descriptive scales, not required scope.
- **01.06 — Introduction to Systems Thinking**
- **01.07 — Isolated Ideas vs Interconnected Worlds**
- **01.08 — Introduction to Creative Compounding**
- **01.09 — Defining Your Own Worldbuilding Goals**

### Practical application

**Assignment — Discover Your Worldbuilding Direction:** Examine a fictional world and define your creative interests and goals.
**Quiz — Worldbuilding Fundamentals:** Check conceptual discrimination and scenario reasoning, not memorized names alone.

**Cumulative handoff:** Reuse this module's work in the following module rather than start a fresh fictional world.

- **02.09 — Avoiding Early Worldbuilding Overcomplexity**
- **02.10 — Creating Your First World Premise**

### Practical application

## 03 — World Foundations & Constraints

**Reasoning:** A world needs baseline assumptions and generative limits before contradictions become meaningful.
**Main objective:** Establish starting conditions, possible and impossible events, and coherent exceptions.

### Lessons

- **03.01 — What Are World Foundations?**
- **03.02 — Defining the World's Starting Conditions**
- **03.03 — Understanding World Rules**
- **03.04 — Physical, Natural, and Environmental Rules**
- **03.05 — Technology, Magic, and Extraordinary Rules**
- **03.06 — Resources, Needs, and Availability**
- **03.07 — Possibilities and Limitations**
- **03.08 — What Are Creative Constraints?**
- **03.09 — Using Constraints to Generate Possibilities**
- **03.10 — General Rules vs Local Exceptions**
- **03.11 — Testing Internal World Consistency**

### Practical application

**Assignment — Establish Your World's Foundations:** State rules and constraints and justify one exception or boundary case.
**Quiz — World Foundations & Constraints:** Check conceptual discrimination and scenario reasoning, not memorized names alone.

**Cumulative handoff:** Reuse this module's work in the following module rather than start a fresh fictional world.

## 04 — People, Places & Lived Experiences

**Main objective:** Create meaningful locations, inhabitants, relationships and everyday consequences.

- **04.10 — Micro, Meso, and Macro Perspectives**
- **04.11 — Translating World Rules into Everyday Life**

**Main objective:** Design interconnected systems and explain how their interactions shape the world.

- **05.08 — Cause and Effect Between World Systems**
- **05.09 — Introduction to Feedback Loops**
- **05.10 — Cross-Domain Worldbuilding**
- **05.11 — Mapping a Small Interconnected System**

### Practical application

## 08 — Canon & Creative Compound Workflows

**Reasoning:** Having designed and expressed a world, learners must preserve coherence and independently generate a second cycle.
**Main objective:** Maintain, evaluate and expand a world using repeatable creative workflows.

### Lessons

- **08.01 — Worldbuilding as an Ongoing Process**
- **08.02 — What Is Canon?**
- **08.03 — Draft Ideas vs Established World Facts**
- **08.04 — Organizing Worldbuilding Knowledge**
- **08.05 — Creating a Simple World Source of Truth**
- **08.06 — Tracking World Rules and Dependencies**
- **08.07 — Maintaining Timeline and Continuity**
- **08.08 — Finding and Resolving Contradictions**
- **08.09 — Revising Ideas Without Losing Context**
- **08.10 — The Creative Compound Machine** — Reconstruct seed → system → interaction → consequence → changed state → new question.
- **08.11 — Turning Previous Outputs into New Inputs**
- **08.12 — Running a Repeatable Creative Loop**
- **08.13 — Avoiding Creative Stagnation**
- **08.14 — Planning Independent World Expansion**

### Practical application

**Assignment — Build Your Worldbuilding Workflow:** Organize the world, run a second cycle, and show output becoming input.
**Quiz — Canon & Creative Compound Workflows:** Check conceptual discrimination and scenario reasoning, not memorized names alone.

**Cumulative handoff:** Produce documented, learner-led next-cycle work and final capstone.

## Final Capstone — My Worldbuilding Operating System v1.0

Consolidate the eight module outputs, document the world-state transition, expose at least one reader-facing experience, audit continuity and run one **second** creative cycle whose seed is derived from the first cycle.
