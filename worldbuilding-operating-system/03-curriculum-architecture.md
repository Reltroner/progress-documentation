# 03 — Curriculum Architecture and Dependency Map

**Status:** PROPOSED — 2026-10-14  
**Role:** Define why each module follows the previous module and prevent unnecessary overlap.

## Sequence contract (candidate)

| Module | Reasoning for its position | Main objective | Prerequisite |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 01 — Worldbuilding Fundamentals | Establish a mental model before isolated vocabulary, aesthetics or lore lists. | Understand worldbuilding as the creation of coherent, interconnected fictional worlds. | None |
| 02 — Creative Seeds & World Premise | A bounded premise lets beginners start before scaling into encyclopedic creation. | Transform an initial creative idea into a clear, manageable world premise. | Module 01 |
| 03 — World Foundations & Constraints | A world needs baseline assumptions and generative limits before contradictions become meaningful. | Establish starting conditions, possible and impossible events, and coherent exceptions. | Module 02 |
| 04 — People, Places & Lived Experiences | Abstract rules must become inhabitants' meaningful daily experiences; scale need not precede intimacy. | Create meaningful locations, inhabitants, relationships and everyday consequences. | Module 03 |
| 05 — World Systems & Interactions | Once parts exist, learners can build causal relationships rather than detached lore categories. | Design interconnected systems and explain how their interactions shape the world. | Module 04 |
| 06 — Causality, Change & Consequences | A functioning world must react to action and inherit changes; complication is not arbitrary spectacle. | Model events, decisions, consequences and world-state transitions. | Module 05 |
| 07 — World Expression & Storytelling | Coherent worlds still need appropriate interfaces for readers; a novel is not compulsory. | Express a fictional world through comprehensible, engaging and suitable formats. | Module 06 |
| 08 — Canon & Creative Compound Workflows | Having designed and expressed a world, learners must preserve coherence and independently generate a second cycle. | Maintain, evaluate and expand a world using repeatable creative workflows. | Module 07 |

## Conceptual dependency chain

01 API-style mental model equivalent: **what is worldbuilding?**  
→ 02 creative input and bounded premise  
→ 03 baseline world rules and constraints  
→ 04 agents, places and everyday consequences  
→ 05 interacting systems  
→ 06 causal events and inherited changes  
→ 07 appropriate expression  
→ 08 continuity, governance and repeated operation.

These are **pedagogical dependencies**, not claims that fiction must always be created in strict chronological order. Creators may revisit earlier modules.

## Content ownership and anti-duplication rules

- **01 owns** beginner vocabulary, conceptual framing and the distinction between isolated lore and systems.
- **02 owns** idea selection and premise/scope; not comprehensive physical laws.
- **03 owns** foundational invariants and supported exceptions; not complete community design.
- **04 owns** lived experience, actor motivation and local perspective; not system causal diagrams.
- **05 owns** interaction, dependency, feedback basics and cross-domain relations; not full event simulations.
- **06 owns** state transitions, events, nontrivial consequences, conflicts and emergent questions.
- **07 owns** communicating a world via multiple reader-facing modes; does not require prose-fiction mastery.
- **08 owns** canon status, continuity workflow, feedback and second creative cycle; not specialized deployment tools.

### Controlled overlap

- Systems thinking appears at **01** as introduction, at **05** as model, at **06** as dynamic consequence and at **08** as repeated workflow.
- World rules are defined at **03**, experienced at **04**, linked at **05**, stress-tested at **06**, and registered at **08**.
- Narratives are allowed throughout exercises but formally examined as communication choices at **07**.
- Canon is mentioned as an integrity concern earlier, with operational status/version management taught at **08**.

## Course object model (conceptual only)

- **Course:** one introductory learning journey.
- **Module:** reasoned dependency stage.
- **Lesson:** one bounded concept + example + practice + comprehension check.
- **Assignment:** cumulative world artifact demonstrating application.
- **Quiz:** conceptual and scenario-based understanding check.
- **Capstone:** assembled world model plus independently completed second creative cycle.
- **Enrichment:** optional advanced branches that do not block beginner completion.

No assumption is made about implementation-specific LMS schemas or APIs.

## Scale and candidate counts

- 8 modules.
- 89 named lessons, spread **9 / 10 / 11 / 11 / 11 / 12 / 11 / 14**.
- 8 proposed assignments and 8 quizzes.
- One course capstone.

These counts describe the **v0.1 syllabus inventory**, not published content, estimated hours or frozen scope. Consolidation or splitting is permitted during curriculum review.

## Progression gates (proposals)

- By **02:** learner has an explicit premise and deliberately limited initial scope.
- By **03:** world rules can be stated and potential contradictions identified.
- By **04:** at least one world rule affects one person/community's lived experience.
- By **05:** two systems have a traceable relationship.
- By **06:** one event changes the world state for identifiable reasons.
- By **07:** another reader can understand one relevant world relationship.
- By **08:** learner can independently document and run a second creative cycle.

## Post-beginner extension boundary

Advanced civilizational simulation, long-horizon politics/economics, hard science, large-scale canon operations, and dedicated AI/software tooling are candidate **follow-on courses**, not entrance requirements.
