# 08 — Teaching Case Study: The City of Stored Memories

**Status:** FICTIONAL, NONCANONICAL, ILLUSTRATIVE — 2026-10-14  
**Role:** Show one small idea developing through all eight proposed modules. **This is not Asthortera canon, official Reltroner lore or a required learner setting.**

## Seed and premise

**What if a city could preserve personally experienced memories in luminous lanterns?**

A town called **Lumenhaven** has developed memory-lantern craft. A lantern allows a person to store a bounded personal recollection and — under specified conditions — share it with another. The case explores memory, trust, consent and evidence without assuming the society is morally perfect.

## Module 01 — Fundamentals

Recognize that “lanterns hold memories” is a **creative seed**, not a complete world. The world requires rules, choices, institutions and consequences. Learners distinguish its setting facts from a possible story.

## Module 02 — Premise and scope

**World premise:** A city of memory artisans preserves personal recollections in handcrafted lanterns, but the difference between remembering and proving becomes increasingly important.

**Scope:** one city, two institutions, a few characters, one contested event. No global map, galaxy lore or ancient pantheon is needed.

## Module 03 — Foundational constraints

- Storing a memory requires the subject's direct participation.
- Sharing a stored memory requires the subject's recorded authorization; an exception must be explained rather than assumed.
- Lanterns preserve a person's experienced recollection, **not objective historical truth**.
- Copies may exist, but the city's normal verification method tracks authorized transfer.
- Memories can be incomplete or sincerely mistaken.

These are **internal fictional rules**, not scientific claims. A later case may test forged permission or broken verification without changing the baseline silently.

## Module 04 — Places, people and daily life

- **Lantern Quarter:** workshops maintain family and community archives.
- **Tess:** a craftsperson who sees lanterns as a continuity and care tool.
- **Iro:** a school archivist who uses authorized recordings to teach local history.
- **Mara:** a dispute mediator who must distinguish memory from corroborated fact.

Daily life: families preserve a grandparent's voice, students learn through historical testimony, and some residents choose not to store experiences. All actors retain meaningful agency.

## Module 05 — Two interacting systems

**Memory-craft system:** crafting standards, consent records, authenticity seals, restoration work.

**Civic-evidence system:** archiving, testimony, contested records, dispute resolution.

**Dependency:** courts increasingly rely on verification from memory artisans; artisans face pressure to prioritize legally admissible records over private family preservation. Rules about trust affect both systems.

## Module 06 — World change simulation

**S0 — before:** archive seals are accepted as trustworthy; family and civic use coexist.

**A — event:** a lantern used to settle a property dispute is revealed to have a valid transfer seal but an edited explanatory label. The recollection itself was not false, but its meaning was misrepresented.

**First-order consequence:** civic mediators suspend reliance on uncorroborated lantern annotations.

**Second-order consequence:** artisans create a transparent annotation provenance standard; costs and delays rise; community groups debate access.

**S1 — after:** a new memory-verification body is proposed, but residents contest who may serve on it.

**Inherited next question:** Can an officially verified memory become an instrument of selective institutional trust?

The next question derives from **S1**, not a randomly added rival country or supervillain.

## Module 07 — Choose the presentation interface

- **Present-event scene:** Mara questions a seal inspector during a public hearing.
- **Backstory fragment:** why the original consent practice gained legitimacy.
- **World reference:** how lantern authorization and provenance actually work.

A learner may choose **one** of these as the first accessible artifact. No novel is necessary.

## Module 08 — Canon/source of truth

Mark the city's five original rules as **structured draft facts**, the hearing outcome as a **proposed event**, and the verification body as **unresolved future architecture**. After reviewing continuity, run the second creative cycle from its consequence: local groups seek alternative community verification processes.

## Counterexample

Weak expansion: “Five new empires arrive with ten legendary lantern types.” It could be entertaining, but nothing shows why prior rules or events generated it.

Stronger expansion: “The annotation standard favors literate institutions; a community develops a new oral attestation method, challenging the original rule's social implementation.” The existing world creates the new problem.

## Teaching constraints

Do not copy this example into learners' deliverables as their required canon. Allow criticism of any design choice; propose alternatives and ask learners to trace different causal outcomes.
