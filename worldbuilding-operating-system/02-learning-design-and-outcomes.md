# 02 — Learning Design, Prerequisites and Outcomes

**Status:** PROPOSED — 2026-10-14  
**Role:** Backward-design the beginner experience from demonstrable learner outcomes.

## Teaching thesis

Learning follows **Understand → Imagine → Define → Populate → Connect → Simulate → Express → Operate**. Introduce creative compounding conceptually in Module 01, reinforce it through cumulative assignments, and teach the integrated workflow in Module 08. This is analogous to using Postman throughout an API course before studying reusable workflows in depth.

## Prerequisites

**Required:** interest in fictional worlds, willingness to imagine and revise, basic reading/writing or equivalent expressive ability. **Not required:** programming, drawing, art history, academic theory, geopolitics, published fiction, AI access or knowledge of Asthortera.

## Course-level learning outcomes (CLOs)

By completion, learners can:

- **CLO-01 Explain:** distinguish fictional world, lore, story, rules and systems using a concrete example.
- **CLO-02 Formulate:** create a bounded original premise and a generative question.
- **CLO-03 Specify:** state key initial conditions, world rules, limits and justified exceptions.
- **CLO-04 Humanize:** show how the setting changes local agents' choices and everyday experience.
- **CLO-05 Connect:** map two interacting systems and a nontrivial dependency.
- **CLO-06 Simulate:** trace an event across initial state, decisions, consequences and new state.
- **CLO-07 Express:** choose an appropriate presentation mode for a specific reader knowledge need.
- **CLO-08 Govern:** label drafts, established facts, unresolved contradictions and revisions.
- **CLO-09 Compound:** demonstrate that one meaningful cycle's output becomes input for a second cycle.
- **CLO-10 Transfer:** articulate and independently use the method without mandatory tools or mentor support.

## Progressive abstraction

1. **Micro:** one person, choice, place or day; direct experiential consequences.
2. **Meso:** community, institution, local resource or cultural interaction.
3. **Macro:** broader system conditions and cross-domain consequences **only when the chosen world requires it**.

Do not confuse increasing scale with increasing quality. A small world can demonstrate sophisticated causal reasoning.

## Proposed lesson pattern

1. Anchor: relatable creator problem or fictional scenario.
2. Concept: one bounded mental model.
3. Worked example: trace the mechanism without large lore dumps.
4. Guided practice: change one assumption and predict an effect.
5. Independent application: add/revise one part of the learner's world.
6. Check: a short question or explain-your-reasoning prompt.
7. Handoff: identify which output becomes input to the next lesson.

## Assessment architecture

- **Formative:** in-lesson checks and reflection prompts; mistakes invite revision.
- **Module summative:** one cumulative assignment plus concept/scenario quiz.
- **Course summative:** learner-owned capstone and independent next-cycle demonstration.
- **Evidence:** inspectable work products and explanations, not merely page views or quiz-click completion.

See [06-assessment-and-capstone.md](06-assessment-and-capstone.md) for draft rubrics. No certified score threshold has been approved.

## Beginner-friendliness and accessibility

- Explain terms before abbreviation; provide optional deeper material.
- Use a single small example world throughout, labeled noncanonical.
- Supply paper/plain-text and Markdown alternatives to diagrams.
- Make exercises compatible with asynchronous written interaction.
- Avoid required audio-only/video-only assessments; provide text alternatives.
- Use supportive, specific feedback rather than evaluating a learner's taste or worldview.
- Do not assume geographic, religious, financial or political assumptions are universal.

## Separation of teaching and ideology

Systems thinking is a method, not a mandate for morally simple worlds. Creator choice governs genre, material conditions, themes and philosophical conclusions. Instructors can ask about benefits, trade-offs and consequences without forcing Reltroner/Asthortera answers.

## Learning efficacy validation

Before claiming success, test whether a novice can independently complete CLO-05, CLO-06 and CLO-09, explain their reasoning, and repeat the process after a break. Completion rates or positive feedback alone are insufficient proof of skill transfer.
