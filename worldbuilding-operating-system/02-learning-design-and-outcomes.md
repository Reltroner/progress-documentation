# 02 — Learning Design, Prerequisites and Outcomes

**Status:** PROPOSED — 2026-10-14  
**Role:** Backward-design the beginner experience from demonstrable learner outcomes.

## Teaching thesis

Learning follows **Encounter a relevant creative use → articulate a mental model → apply it in worldbuilding → test a bounded analogy → revise the model**. The eight-module domain sequence remains **Understand → Imagine → Define → Populate → Connect → Simulate → Express → Operate**. Creative compounding begins as a small demonstration in Module 01 and matures into independent workflow in Module 08. Tool-specific software-course analogies are not a pedagogical prerequisite.

## Prerequisites

**Required:** interest in fictional worlds, willingness to imagine and revise, basic reading/writing or equivalent expressive ability. **Not required:** programming, drawing, art history, academic theory, geopolitics, published fiction, AI access or knowledge of Asthortera.

## Course-level learning outcomes (CLOs)

By completion, learners can:

- **CLO-01 Explain:** distinguish fictional world, lore, story, rules and systems using a concrete example.
- **CLO-02 Formulate:** create a bounded original premise and a generative question.
- **CLO-03 Specify:** state key initial conditions, world rules, limits and justified exceptions.
- **CLO-04 Humanize:** show how the setting changes local agents' choices and everyday experience.
- **CLO-05 Connect:** map two interacting systems and a nontrivial dependency.
- **CLO-06 Simulate:** trace an event across initial state, decisions, consequences and new state.
- **CLO-07 Express:** choose an appropriate presentation mode for a specific reader knowledge need.
- **CLO-08 Govern:** label drafts, established facts, unresolved contradictions and revisions.
- **CLO-09 Compound:** demonstrate that one meaningful cycle's output becomes input for a second cycle.
- **CLO-10 Independent reuse:** articulate and independently reapply the method in a different fictional world, genre or creative task without mandatory tools or mentor support.
- **CLO-11 Bounded cross-domain transfer:** adapt one learned reasoning pattern to an unfamiliar, low-risk **non-fiction** problem, explicitly identifying what maps, what breaks, required evidence and a reversible test.
- **CLO-12 Metacognitive calibration:** compare two plausible explanations or interventions, identify uncertainty and revise an initial decision in response to new evidence.

## Backward design: usage → mental model → transfer evidence (candidate v0.3)

The **usage** is a question the learner actually has or a clearly hypothetical problem worth exploring; the **mental model** is an explicitly reusable representation, not a list of worldbuilding terms. Students must first see the method solve a small fictional problem before being invited to test its limits in another context.

For every future lesson, record:

1. **Problem to solve:** one understandable fictional-world situation and a decision at stake.
2. **Model in one sentence:** e.g. constraint/trade-off, system boundary, actor incentives, dependency chain, feedback, perspective, state transition, or reversible experiment.
3. **Application:** what a learner changes in their own fictional world; attach an inspectable cause-and-effect trace.
4. **Transfer probe:** a *different* low-risk context; specify matching structure, nonmatching facts and missing real-world evidence.
5. **Reflection:** what changed in the learner's reasoning and which alternative explanation was considered.

**Scaffolding:** Module 01–02 = notice/describe an analogy (optional); Module 03–04 = guided adaptation; Module 05–07 = reason with new contextual assumptions and compare alternatives; Module 08 = independently select and justify a mental model in one previously unseen non-fiction scenario. Such progression is a **proposed instructional design**, not yet validated.

**Separately assess:** (a) worldbuilding competence, (b) identifying the abstract pattern, (c) near transfer inside fiction, (d) *farther* transfer into a real everyday context. Passing (a) does not imply (d). Do not infer transfer from quiz recall, enthusiastic feedback, or fictional artifacts alone. Explicit mapping: [14](14-usage-mental-model-and-transfer-map.md).

## Progressive abstraction

1. **Micro:** one person, choice, place or day; direct experiential consequences.
2. **Meso:** community, institution, local resource or cultural interaction.
3. **Macro:** broader system conditions and cross-domain consequences **only when the chosen world requires it**.

Do not confuse increasing scale with increasing quality. A small world can demonstrate sophisticated causal reasoning.

## Proposed lesson pattern

1. Anchor: relatable creator problem or fictional scenario.
2. Concept: one bounded mental model.
3. Worked example: trace the mechanism without large lore dumps.
4. Guided practice: change one assumption and predict an effect.
5. Independent application: add/revise one part of the learner's world.
6. Check: a short question or explain-your-reasoning prompt.
7. Handoff: identify which output becomes input to the next lesson.

## Progressive-disclosure lesson contract

| Level | Job | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| L1 | Human hook | What happens after an exciting idea? |
| L2 | Familiar difficulty | More lore does not explain what changed |
| L3 | Bounded value | Reuse a consequence to find the next question |
| L4 | Worked method | Seed → constraint → interaction → consequence → changed state → new question |
| L5 | Optional detail | Causal dependencies, governance and world states |

L1–L5 describes **communication depth**, not module numbers. Demonstrate a small choice and consequence **before abstraction** where possible; then give a learner-owned micro-task, an honest limitation and a voluntary deeper route.

The **six-stage practice** from [Communication Framework §11](https://github.com/Reltroner/reltroner-studio/blob/main/content/principles/reltroner-studio-communication-framework.md) is a beginner-facing translation of [Master §47](https://github.com/Reltroner/reltroner-studio/blob/main/content/principles/reltroner-studio-master-source-of-truth.md): seed → system → interaction → consequence → changed state → new pressure → new question → possibility → further interaction. The constraint helps define system limits; pressure and possibilities can be unfolded later. Neither list is a universal physics law. A learner's explanation of a supplied example is weaker evidence than an independently completed second cycle.

## Assessment architecture

- **Formative:** in-lesson checks and reflection prompts; mistakes invite revision.
- **Module summative:** one cumulative assignment plus concept/scenario quiz.
- **Course summative:** learner-owned capstone and independent next-cycle demonstration.
- **Evidence:** inspectable work products and explanations, not merely page views or quiz-click completion.

See [06-assessment-and-capstone.md](06-assessment-and-capstone.md) for draft rubrics. No certified score threshold has been approved.

## Beginner-friendliness and accessibility

- Explain terms before abbreviation; provide optional deeper material.
- Use a single small example world throughout, labeled noncanonical.
- Supply paper/plain-text and Markdown alternatives to diagrams.
- Make exercises compatible with asynchronous written interaction.
- Avoid required audio-only/video-only assessments; provide text alternatives.
- Use supportive, specific feedback rather than evaluating a learner's taste or worldview.
- Do not assume geographic, religious, financial or political assumptions are universal.

## Separation of teaching and ideology

Systems thinking is a method, not a mandate for morally simple worlds. Creator choice governs genre, material conditions, themes and philosophical conclusions. Instructors can ask about benefits, trade-offs and consequences without forcing Reltroner/Asthortera answers.

## Learning efficacy validation

Before claiming success, test whether a novice can independently complete CLO-05, CLO-06 and CLO-09, explain their reasoning, and repeat the process after a break. Completion rates or positive feedback alone are insufficient proof of skill transfer.
