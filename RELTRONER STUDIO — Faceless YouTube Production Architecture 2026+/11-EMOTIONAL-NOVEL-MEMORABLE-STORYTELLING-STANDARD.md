# 11 — Emotional, Novel, and Memorable Storytelling Standard

**Version:** 1.0-draft  
**Baseline:** 2026-11-03  
**Classification:** Proposed editorial and media-production standard; subordinate to the binding Reltroner Studio master source of truth  
**Inspiration:** Carmine Gallo, *Talk Like TED: The 9 Public-Speaking Secrets of the World's Top Minds* (2014)  
**Applies to:** Reltroner Studio faceless Shorts, explainer videos, longer essays, audio direction, thumbnails, scripts, site previews, and related community-facing excerpts  
**Status:** Editorial architecture, not a verified video outcome, canon revision, platform guarantee, or monetization claim

## 1. Purpose and positioning

Reltroner Studio's existing communication doctrine prioritizes relevance, causal explanation, evidence, recipient autonomy, and optional depth. This standard adds an **emotional, novel, memorable (ENM) delivery layer** to improve the viewer's ability to *feel why an idea matters, discover a genuinely new perspective, and carry away a coherent insight*.

The ENM layer does not replace the Creative Compound Machine, Asthortera, the studio's narrative authority, the source hierarchy, or the communication framework. It shapes the *presentation* of authorized ideas. Communication remains truthful and context-sensitive even when vivid.

The distinction matters: emotional resonance is not permission to manipulate; novelty is not permission to invent; memorability is not permission to reduce complex fiction to a false slogan.

## 2. Source foundation and correct adaptation

Carmine Gallo's *Talk Like TED* synthesizes features of effective presentations under three broad organizing elements: **emotional**, **novel**, and **memorable**. Gallo's public discussion describes emotional engagement, new learning/surprise, and techniques that help ideas stick. The book also discusses nine public-speaking practices, including genuine conviction, storytelling, conversational delivery, learning something new, high-impact moments, appropriate lightness, concise structure, multi-sensory mental pictures, and authenticity.

**Sources:**
- Author's book overview: https://www.carminegallo.com/books/talk-like-ted/
- Author's explanation of the three components: https://www.carminegallo.com/the-3-things-the-worlds-best-presentations-have-in-common-salesforce-com-blog/
- Publisher description: https://read.macmillan.com/lp/talk-like-ted-by-carmine-gallo/

**Adaptation limits:**
- This document paraphrases and operationalizes general principles; it does not reproduce book chapters or imply affiliation with the author or TED.
- TED stagecraft does not map literally to a 55-second vertical video. The book's 18-minute principle should be interpreted here as *respect for viewer attention and proportionate duration*, not an 18-minute requirement or a scientifically universal attention limit.
- The nine techniques are a creative checklist, not nine mandatory effects per video.
- Emotional or “jaw-dropping” moments must arise from the real topic and authorized story, not forced spectacle.
- Performance hypotheses require actual channel data, not neuroscience claims or assumed improvements in algorithmic distribution.

## 3. Three-part editorial contract

| Dimension | Viewer question | Required editorial outcome | Prohibited shortcut |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **E — Emotional relevance** | Why should this matter to a human being? | A recognizable desire, tension, loss, choice, fear, wonder, relief, or unresolved question | Fearmongering, fabricated stakes, manipulative trauma, fake founder/character testimony |
| **N — Genuine novelty** | What unfamiliar but defensible idea will I learn or reconsider? | One new mechanism, contrast, consequence, reframing, or world rule grounded in applicable sources | Unsupported rankings, fabricated facts, AI hallucination, clickbait contradiction |
| **M — Durable memorability** | What single idea, image, or causal sequence should remain afterward? | One honest takeaway reinforced by a distinctive visual, sonic, or narrative anchor | Endless slogans, gratuitous effects, misleading over-simplification |

**Default balance:** Emotion opens the door; novelty gives the viewer a reason to stay; memorability helps the viewer retain the meaning. This is not a fixed three-act formula or a guaranteed causal model of audience behavior. The order can vary to suit the story.

## 4. Emotional design: experience before encyclopedia

### 4.1 Human stake first

For reader-facing videos, start from a choice, vulnerable assumption, everyday consequence, or curiosity that someone unfamiliar with Asthortera can recognize. Examples include:
- What would belonging mean when basic material needs are abundant?
- Can a society protect memory without controlling what people are allowed to know?
- Can comfort sustain people while also concealing a dangerous dependency?

For creator-facing videos:
- A creator has an exciting world but cannot find the next meaningful event.
- An idea produces an unintended consequence, creating a new conflict.
- A fictional institution seems flawless until someone must live inside it.

This translates an abstract system into a human experience without declaring any unratified fact about real or fictional populations.

### 4.2 Emotional palette and restraint

Choose one primary feeling and one purposeful emotional shift per short episode, where appropriate:

| Primary feeling | Appropriate motion/audio | Common failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Curiosity | Quiet opening question, unusual contrast, sparse cue | Empty mystery without explanation |
| Wonder | Scale revealed gradually, breathing room, restrained ambience | Excessive cosmic spectacle without meaning |
| Unease | Subtle tension, an institutional contradiction, a pause | Horror effects that misrepresent the theme |
| Empathy | Specific choice or dilemma, intimate narration, gentle pacing | Invented suffering or false testimony |
| Recognition | Familiar creative frustration, small demonstration | Patronizing the audience |
| Relief/possibility | A visible causal resolution or next step | False guarantee or promotional euphoria |

Do not assume dramatic music or synthetic-voice emotional exaggeration is necessary. Silence and restrained delivery can communicate more accurately.

### 4.3 Ethics gate for emotion

The emotional hook must be true to the authorized creative material. Do not:
- invent a tragic event for attention;
- exploit sensitive real-world issues merely to trigger anger;
- imply the fictional civilization is an Earth country;
- present non-canonical visuals or speculative futures as released canon;
- manufacture urgency or false scarcity;
- use an identifiable real person's synthetic voice without consent.

**Editorial test:** Could the founder defend the emotional framing as a fair interpretation of this exact source even if the video were watched without its thumbnail?

## 5. Novelty design: surprising, but still grounded

Novelty can be created by:
1. **Expectation inversion:** a spacefaring civilization does not need a cyberpunk appearance.
2. **Causal reveal:** archival practices create political or institutional consequences beyond the building's aesthetic.
3. **Unexpected scale translation:** show a comprehensible personal consequence before a large canonical statistic.
4. **Cross-domain analogy:** explain a creative constraint through governance, architecture, culture, or technology—while labeling analogies.
5. **Mechanism demonstration:** an existing idea becomes an input to a new creative question.
6. **Perspective shift:** show the same rule from a reader's, citizen's, and creator's viewpoint.

The novelty claim must be **new to the intended viewer**, not necessarily objectively unprecedented globally. Never say “nobody has thought of this,” “the world's first,” “the most advanced,” or “scientifically proven” without appropriate evidence.

## 6. Memorable design: one message and one anchor

### 6.1 One-sentence thesis

Before storyboarding, write an honest takeaway in one sentence. This is a working editorial discipline, not a forced slogan. Example:

**“An advanced civilization's values can shape the appearance of its technology more deeply than its technological power alone.”**

This is an interpretive thesis for a conceptual Depcutland video, not a newly ratified canonical definition.

### 6.2 Three-beat story memory

Where useful, compress the video to three coherent beats:

**Familiar expectation → Documented fictional contrast → Human consequence or question**

Do not mechanically create three bullets in every shot. The “rule of three” is a presentational option, not a canon rule or a retention guarantee.

### 6.3 Memorable audiovisual anchor

A candidate memory anchor may be:
- a door opens onto a silent monumental archive;
- one restrained sound marks the shift from familiar neon to archival civilization;
- an on-screen diagram shows “idea → consequence → next question”;
- a recurring visual motif, such as a threshold, an unresolved line, or a quiet architectural reveal.

**Anchor standard:** repeat meaning, not noise. No recurring effect is allowed to override intelligibility, captions, accuracy, source rights, or the studio's visual character.

### 6.4 Sound as memory, not decoration

Connect each central insight to an authorized sound event or intentional silence:
- Hook: optional low impact to mark a question.
- Novel contrast: a restrained acoustic transition, not a deafening rise.
- Mechanism: sound follows the actual visual event, such as a node linking.
- Conclusion: a shorter quiet pause can give the viewer cognitive room.

The sound cue must be declared in the scene-relative audio manifest and reviewed in the final mix. Narrative goals do not override loudness, clipping, copyright, voice clarity, or budget gates.

## 7. Faceless adaptation of Gallo's presentation ideas

| Presentation idea | Faceless Reltroner implementation | Verification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Genuine conviction | Specific original question and founder-approved viewpoint; no fake first-person autobiography spoken by TTS | Source and author approval |
| Storytelling | Human dilemma, decision and consequence before encyclopedic lore | Script review |
| Conversational delivery | Natural Indonesian syntax, voice casting and tested pacing | Listen to real TTS output |
| Teach something new | Distinctive causal mechanism grounded in lore or method | Source traceability |
| Strong reveal | Meaningful visual contrast or consequence, not artificially inflated shock | Canon/visual review |
| Appropriate lightness | Context-sensitive levity without undermining serious themes | Editorial review |
| Attention-respecting structure | Length proportional to one idea and actual voice fit | Timed narration; retention only later |
| Multisensory imagery | Picture, motion, voice, ambient sound, and silence reinforcing the same point | Full playback review |
| Authenticity | Keep Reltroner's genuine aesthetic, doubts, and creator agency visible | Founder approval |

This is an adaptation of general lessons, not a reproduction of the book's prose.

## 8. Production integration: ENM as separate data, not a separate renderer

Do not create a new video rendering engine. Add **optional proposed editorial metadata** to the existing episode and scene manifest scheme. These fields are a specification example, not an implemented validated schema:

~~~json
{
  "storytelling": {
    "schemaVersion": "enm-1.0-proposed",
    "humanQuestion": "Must the future always look cyberpunk?",
    "primaryEmotion": "curiosity",
    "emotionalShift": "curiosity-to-wonder",
    "expectedAssumption": "Advanced technology must look visibly futuristic.",
    "novelReframe": "Archival institutions and cultural priorities shape the visual language of technology.",
    "singleSentenceTakeaway": "Technological maturity does not dictate one visual aesthetic.",
    "memoryAnchorSceneId": "SC-002",
    "memoryAnchorCueId": "SFX-REVEAL-001",
    "noveltyEvidenceRefs": ["SRC-DEPCUTLAND-001"],
    "editorialClassification": "interpretive-proposal",
    "enmApproval": "PENDING"
  }
}
~~~

A scene may optionally declare:

~~~json
{
  "sceneId": "SC-002",
  "storyRole": "contrast-reveal",
  "viewerQuestion": "What if advanced technology looked like a library?",
  "emotionIntent": "wonder",
  "noveltyFunction": "expectation-inversion",
  "memoryAnchor": true,
  "voiceDeliveryNote": "quiet, conversational, unhurried",
  "audioIntent": "soft indoor acoustic reveal",
  "preserveOnRevision": [
    "narration.text",
    "audioCues",
    "durationFrames",
    "sourceRefs"
  ]
}
~~~

**Compatibility gate:** if the initial validator has not implemented these fields, store them in a separate, versioned editorial brief rather than adding undocumented objects and declaring the manifest valid.

## 9. Timing, audio and per-scene revision contract

ENM reviews do not nullify the existing selective-revision policy. A visual or audio revision must identify **what emotional or mnemonic role is being changed**, then define invariants and dependencies.

Example:

~~~text
EPISODE: depcutland-001
SCENE: SC-002
SCOPE: emotional contrast and visual pacing only
REQUEST: Reduce the suddenness of the reveal; introduce the classical archive
through a deliberate, quiet transition that supports wonder rather than shock.
PRESERVE: original approved voice words; source claims; durationFrames;
other scenes; licensed SFX; global loudness and clip-free mix.
VALIDATE: mobile legibility, visual-to-cue alignment, contrast readability,
human listen/watch review, and no unintended timeline shift.
DO NOT: fabricate a new Depcutland artifact, publish, use paid APIs, or
re-voice unrelated scenes.
~~~

If an edit affects narrator duration, neighboring transitions, or global audio envelopes, recompute all dependent artifacts and disclose the broader change.

## 10. Editorial quality gate — ENM-01 to ENM-12

| ID | Review question | Pass criteria |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ENM-01 | Is there a specific human curiosity or stake? | Viewers can recognize the question without learning internal terms first |
| ENM-02 | Does the emotional tone fit the source? | No invented suffering, rage, or forced sentiment |
| ENM-03 | Is the story understandable without prior lore? | First-contact comprehension passes human review |
| ENM-04 | What is genuinely new? | One distinct source-supported insight, not just novel effects |
| ENM-05 | Is the surprise fairly framed? | No false global rankings, misleading imagery, or fake facts |
| ENM-06 | Is there one explainable takeaway? | A reviewer can articulate the point without repeating jargon |
| ENM-07 | Does a memorable anchor serve the idea? | Distinctive image/sound adds meaning rather than distraction |
| ENM-08 | Is synthetic delivery conversational and clear? | Actual listened sample has natural pacing and valid pronunciation |
| ENM-09 | Does audio reinforce, not overwhelm? | Cues align with visual events and narration remains legible |
| ENM-10 | Are sources/status/rights intact? | Canon/evidence class and licenses are recorded |
| ENM-11 | Does it respect time and viewer autonomy? | No information dump, coercive CTA, or manufactured scarcity |
| ENM-12 | Was a human reviewer involved? | Explicit approval or revision evidence exists |

Permissible outcomes: **PASS / REVISE / DEFER / NOT APPLICABLE**. A polished AI draft does not constitute PASS.

## 11. A/B learning hypotheses, without invented outcome data

Compare candidate creative executions *only after sufficient actual production and audience evidence exists*. Hypotheses might be:
- **Human-first hook vs aesthetic-first hook:** which better earns meaningful attention?
- **One visual contrast vs a dense statistics panel:** which helps viewers understand the central mechanism?
- **Quiet sound reveal vs loud impact:** which supports the intended emotion without compromising speech?
- **One-sentence close vs extended lore CTA:** which leads to more informed, voluntary exploration?

Do not call the result an algorithmic rule. Segmentation, sample size, packaging, upload timing, and source subject can confound comparisons. Avoid claiming neuroscience or retention multipliers without robust evidence.

## 12. Depcutland worked creative treatment (proposed only)

**Working topic:** Why Must the Future Look Cyberpunk?  
**Primary emotion:** curiosity shifting to wonder, ending in reflection  
**Novel premise:** advancement and architecture need not share one global visual style  
**Memorable takeaway:** a civilization's deepest technology may be embedded in how it organizes knowledge, not merely in how its buildings glow  
**Visual anchor:** familiar neon city gives way to a quiet, monumental neoclassical archive  
**Sonic anchor:** abrupt exterior urban noise gently dissolves into spacious archival ambience; the transition is modest and intentional  
**Ethical constraint:** do not treat the generated visual as literal canon; any unverified fictional specifics remain illustrations/interpretation.

Suggested six-scene mapping:

| Scene | Role | Emotion | Novelty/memory | Audio intention |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SC-001 | Familiar default | Curiosity | Ask why future imagery looks uniform | Gentle city bed; no harsh clickbait impact |
| SC-002 | Visual expectation inversion | Wonder | Cyberpunk-to-archive contrast | Soft acoustic reveal |
| SC-003 | Anchor in Depcutland | Orientation | Introduce epistemic/archival priorities | Quiet interior texture |
| SC-004 | Show causal mechanism | Understanding | Values/institutions shape visible technology | Subtle event-locked cue |
| SC-005 | Human consequence/question | Reflection | Ask what progress means to inhabitants | Space and intentional pause |
| SC-006 | One takeaway + invitation | Resolution/curiosity | Preserve one memorable thought | Soft fade, optional relevant link |

**Do not change approved narration merely to heighten feeling.** Any script rewrite is a distinct proposal requiring source review, voice regeneration, timing checks, and human acceptance.

## 13. Integration with Reltroner's creative engines

**Creator-facing:** reveal the creative loop through a concrete obstacle and consequence. Emotional recognition (“I have ideas but cannot continue them”), novelty (one constraint creates a useful next question), and memory (a concise causal diagram) are communication outputs; they do not claim guaranteed productivity.

**Reader-facing:** begin with a lived question, reveal a fictional contrast, and end on a meaningful human or institutional consequence. A non-canon illustrative scene remains clearly non-canon even if visually compelling.

**Cross-cutting:** an approved video can become a source for a new discussion or learning exercise, but should never automatically rewrite the underlying fiction or declare commercial validation.
